We study environment-assisted quantum transport (ENAQT) in tight-binding chains with site-local pure dephasing, an irreversible trap and uniform excitation loss, computing the trapping yield eta(gamma) exactly within the Lindblad model from a single linear solve per parameter point. For a two-site donor-acceptor pair the dephasing enhancement eta_peak/eta_zero grows with the energy bias, from 1.06 at eps = 0.5 Delta to 7.20 at eps = 5 Delta, with the optimal dephasing rate tracking the bias, gamma* ~ eps. For linear energy funnels of N=2-20 sites the optimal rate falls as gamma* ~ N^-1.20 while the peak yield falls from 0.80 to 0.25. The enhancement ratio itself is dominated by its reference point: measured against gamma = 1e-3 Delta it grows linearly up to N=10 and plateaus near 38, whereas against the coherent limit gamma=0 it keeps growing to 1.3e3 at N=20. In an ensemble of 1,000 chains with Gaussian site disorder (sigma = 2 Delta, N=2-15), 97.4% of realizations show an interior yield maximum with at least 1% gain. Median enhancement ratios exceed those of the ordered funnel at large N only because disorder suppresses the coherent baseline; the absolute peak yield of disordered chains is lower at every N. Optimizing site energies for the largest ratio drives the chain to a two-level step profile whose peak yield is again below that of the funnel. For the seven-site FMO Hamiltonian with the trap at BChl 3 we find a 4.7-fold enhancement at gamma* = 1.9 Delta_FMO, of the same order as a high-temperature estimate of the protein dephasing rate. We conclude that peak yield and optimal dephasing rate, not the enhancement ratio, are the robust figures of merit for noise-assisted transport.
