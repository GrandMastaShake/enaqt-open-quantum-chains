We study environment-assisted quantum transport (ENAQT) in tight-binding chains with site-local pure dephasing, an irreversible trap and uniform excitation loss, and compute the trapping yield exactly. For linear energy funnels of N=2-20 sites at fixed total bias, the optimal dephasing rate equals the nearest-neighbour energy mismatch, gamma* = eps_tot/(N-1), to within 2%, the trapped-chain counterpart of the rule found for boundary-driven tilted chains; it maximizes the Haken-Strobl-Reineker hopping rate on every bond at once and holds to within 15% over two decades of trap and loss rates. Because the dephasing acts at infinite temperature, the yield is exactly invariant under reversing the bias, and a flat chain, which shows no ENAQT, reaches a higher yield than any funnel; dephasing only repairs yield lost to mismatch. The enhancement ratio eta_peak/eta_ref is set by its reference point and by the rates: for eps_tot = 5 Delta, above the bandwidth, it plateaus at 36-38 against gamma = 1e-3 Delta but reaches 1.3e3 at N=20 against the coherent limit, whereas for eps_tot = 2 Delta it stays near 1.6. In 1,000 chains with Gaussian site disorder (sigma = 2 Delta), 97.4% show ENAQT and median ratios exceed the funnel's because the coherent baseline collapses; only 4 of these chains beat the flat chain, while weaker disorder of matched nearest-neighbour strength beats the funnel. Maximizing the ratio over site energies has no finite optimum: it drives the profile to the bounds of the search space and lowers the yield. For the seven-site FMO complex with the trap at BChl 3, gamma* = 1.9 Delta_FMO, of the order of a high-temperature estimate of the protein dephasing rate, while the 4.7-fold enhancement falls to 1.14 at biological trap and loss rates. Peak yield and optimal dephasing rate, not the enhancement ratio, are the robust figures of merit.
